# Category Playbooks

Category knowledge is context, not a replacement for product diagnosis or communication strategy.

## Use
Resolve:
- one primary category when useful,
- an optional secondary category only when it materially changes the design problem.

Category context may influence:
- expected use/scenario cues,
- proof/trust expectations,
- material and lighting treatment,
- information density,
- demonstration style,
- common category clichés,
- opportunities for meaningful differentiation.

## Do not use category as style automation
Avoid rules such as:
- beauty = pink,
- technology = blue,
- premium = black/gold,
- youth = neon.

A category playbook should alter evidence, scene, demonstration, hierarchy, or tone for a reason—not merely assign a visual cliché.

## Brand override
Verified brand rules and approved campaign logic override generic category conventions.

## Category Visual Intelligence chain

Derive the useful visual prior through:

**CATEGORY → SUBCATEGORY → PURCHASE MOTIVATION → USAGE CONTEXT → SENSORY ATTRIBUTE → BRAND POSITIONING → VISUAL GRAMMAR**

Then reconcile that prior with product truth, price tier, audience, platform, communication job, market saturation, and the active Reference Adoption Mapping. Category never outputs a finished color palette or style by itself.

For each project, resolve only the category signals that change:
- product view / scale / camera,
- active usage behavior,
- proof and trust expectations,
- scene and contact requirements,
- light / shadow / material response,
- sensory cues,
- commercial hierarchy,
- clichés to avoid and differentiation opportunities.

## Ten category priors

These priors are tendencies, not templates. Subcategory and product evidence always refine them.

### 1. Smart Home

Core motivations may include effortless assistance, comfort, control, spatial harmony, or calm automation.

Visual grammar tendencies:
- show object–space integration and responsive environmental behavior,
- use light and atmosphere to explain function, not to default to “tech blue”,
- preserve architectural scale and believable room illumination,
- make Usage Hero show a real environmental response or body interaction.

Subcategory differences:
- smart sleep mask: intimate, tactile, low-light, soft material, facial contact, sleep immersion,
- smart speaker: architectural, spatial, ambient, connected, responsive light/sound relationship,
- smart lamp: illumination behavior, desk atmosphere, light falloff, object–space relationship.

Avoid applying one blue futuristic studio to all three.

### 2. Digital Accessories

Core motivations may include precision, portability, speed, reliability, connectivity, or industrial quality.

Visual grammar tendencies:
- macro detail and controlled hard/soft light when materials or interfaces matter,
- camera and geometry that reveal construction, scale, or portability,
- energy/interface/connectivity cues only when they clarify a verified function,
- floating only when conceptually justified and physically legible.

Usage Hero must show real insertion, wearing, charging, plugging, gripping, or transport behavior with credible contact and occlusion. Avoid cheap cyberpunk templates, gratuitous neon, and fictional UI.

### 3. Eco Lifestyle

Core motivations may include reuse, reduced waste, material honesty, lifecycle value, or better everyday habits.

Visual grammar tendencies:
- show fibers, pulp, wood, recycled or durable surfaces when verified,
- favor natural tactility and real repeated-use behavior,
- use lifecycle / refill / storage / cleaning / carry relationships where relevant,
- let color follow material and brand rather than equating sustainability with green.

Usage Hero should show the sustainable behavior in practice, not merely place the product beside leaves.

### 4. Health & Fitness

Core motivations may include movement, recovery, comfort, measurable progress, or ergonomic support.

Visual grammar tendencies:
- body mechanics, pressure, grip, alignment, motion, or recovery context,
- credible anatomy and scale,
- evidence/proof only when verified,
- lighting and crop that make the body–product relationship legible.

Usage Hero is critical: the product must touch, support, move with, or be operated by the body. Avoid generic red/black intensity and decorative motion lines without visible action.

### 5. Beauty & Skincare

Core motivations may include texture, ritual, efficacy, sensorial pleasure, self-image, or ingredient perception.

Visual grammar tendencies:
- resolve glass/plastic/metal and liquid/cream/powder response separately,
- use skin, dispensing, application, absorption, sheen, translucency, viscosity, or ingredient cues when truthful,
- select clinical, luxury, natural, youthful, or editorial expression from positioning and evidence,
- control reflections, highlights, condensation, and surface cleanliness precisely.

Usage Hero must show a real ritual or application moment. Avoid default “pink social-media skincare” styling.

### 6. Fashion & Accessories

Core motivations may include identity, styling, silhouette, material, craft, belonging, or cultural expression.

Visual grammar tendencies:
- make silhouette, drape, fit, styling, or material tactility legible,
- let Usage Hero answer “who am I when I wear/carry this?”,
- use pose and crop to demonstrate fit and movement rather than making the model the only subject,
- preserve accurate product construction, scale, fastening, and body contact.

Avoid generic editorial poses that hide the product or imply an unsupported fit.

### 7. Food & Beverage

Core motivations may include appetite, freshness, temperature, texture, ritual, convenience, or sharing.

Visual grammar tendencies:
- use steam, condensation, melting, liquid dynamics, crumb, crunch, pull, pour, or freshness cues only when product-valid,
- show consumption or preparation rather than only packaging,
- choose camera distance and light for appetite and texture,
- keep packaging identity readable while food/drink remains desirable.

Usage Hero should show pouring, opening, biting, sipping, serving, or preparing. Avoid static pack-only lifestyle scenes when consumption experience is the selling point.

### 8. Home & Living

Core motivations may include spatial order, comfort, cleanliness, organization, convenience, or daily ritual.

Visual grammar tendencies:
- show product–space relationships, before/after potential, storage/containment, or routine,
- preserve believable room scale, placement plane, and object hierarchy,
- let lighting and materials match the actual room function,
- use only props that explain value or scale.

Usage Hero should show organizing, cleaning, storing, preparing, resting, or another real domestic action—not a styled room with an idle product.

### 9. Maternal & Baby

Core motivations may include safety, softness, trust, care, usability, hygiene, and caregiver confidence.

Visual grammar tendencies:
- prioritize truthful contact, gentle material response, ergonomic use, and clean/hygienic cues,
- make adult operation and supervision legible when relevant,
- use color from brand and context rather than fixed pink/blue coding,
- avoid over-cartooning when adult trust is the primary purchase barrier.

Usage Hero must depict a safe, plausible care/feeding/holding/cleaning relationship without inventing safety claims.

### 10. Pet

Core motivations may include health, feeding, hygiene, play, bonding, safety, or companionship.

Visual grammar tendencies:
- show real animal behavior and species-appropriate scale,
- preserve credible muzzle/paw/body contact, fur occlusion, spill/wear traces, and product stability,
- let the pet support the product-use relationship rather than becoming a portrait subject,
- use environment and props to clarify feeding, drinking, play, rest, grooming, or hygiene.

Usage Hero should show the pet actually drinking, eating, playing, entering, resting on, or otherwise using the product. A pet sitting beside the product is insufficient.

## Runtime schema
For a category-specific note, record:
- category,
- typical purchase/use context,
- common viewer questions,
- common proof expectations,
- useful demonstration patterns,
- visual conventions,
- saturated clichés,
- risks,
- differentiation opportunities,
- source/verification note when claims are externally researched.

Do not invent category-specific facts when no category evidence has been supplied or researched.


## Scene validity check

When category/use context materially affects an image, validate the scene before production.

Ask internally:
- what is the product actually doing in this scene?
- is the object scale and placement plausible for the category?
- does the scene support a real purchase/use context, or is it only a literal visualization of campaign copy?
- are there more category-legible contexts that communicate the same campaign idea with less semantic distance?
- does the scene create proof/use meaning, not just atmosphere?

A campaign phrase such as "travel", "autumn", "lightweight", or "outdoor" does not by itself justify a destination landscape or decorative lifestyle background.

For simple utility products, prefer a scene where the product's relation to the user/action/context is legible.

### Daily-use functional product scene priority

For ordinary physical products whose value is understood through daily use — such as drinkware, small home appliances, kitchen tools, storage, commuting goods, wearables, and similar utility products — use this default scene priority for hero and key selling-point visuals:

1. **real use action / interaction**,
2. **credible use-context environment**,
3. **use-adjacent scene with clear contextual cues**,
4. **abstract pedestal / geometric / symbolic stage**.

A person is not mandatory. Realism can also come from credible support, containment, nearby objects, scale cues, occlusion, or environmental traces of use.

Do not default to abstract blocks, pedestals, or decorative geometry merely because they look polished. If an abstract scene is selected for a daily-use functional product, the Visual Direction must state why it serves the communication job better than a credible use context. “Looks premium” is not sufficient by itself.

If the selected scene is primarily symbolic, the Visual Direction should explicitly justify that abstraction.

Scene validity must be handed to Visual Direction / Visual Evidence Strategy / Anchor Production as a constraint, not merely recorded as category research.
