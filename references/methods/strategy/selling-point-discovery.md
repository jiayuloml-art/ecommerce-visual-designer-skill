# Selling Point Discovery

Use this when the project needs to determine what is worth communicating, especially before selecting the hero message or visual proposition.

## Evidence order
Prefer:
1. user-verified facts,
2. official specs/manuals/brand sources,
3. supplied project documents,
4. unambiguous visible information in assets,
5. category/domain knowledge only as hypothesis.

## Evidence typing
Keep three states separate:
- **CONFIRMED FACT**
- **DERIVED BENEFIT**
- **HYPOTHESIS**

Do not present hypotheses as product facts.

## Transformation chain
For each candidate:

**Feature → Advantage → Consumer Benefit → Visual Selling Point**

- Feature: what the product objectively has/does.
- Advantage: why that feature is meaningfully different or useful.
- Consumer Benefit: what changes for the user.
- Visual Selling Point: how that value can be shown quickly and credibly.

## Visual potential
Prioritize candidates that combine:
- consumer value,
- immediate comprehensibility,
- visualizability,
- platform fit,
- memorability,
- direct relation to the product,
- evidence strength.

Do not use a fixed numeric score when qualitative evidence is enough.

## Selection
Default to:
- **1 Hero Selling Point**
- at most **1–2 Supporting Selling Points**

Move additional facts into secondary images, detail modules, comparison, proof, or video rather than forcing everything into the hero.

## Traceability
For any consequential selling-point claim, retain:

**Evidence → Product Condition → Visual/Message Decision → Expected Effect → Risk/Trade-off**

In CLIENT MODE, expose only the concise recommendation and material reason.
