# Visual Benchmarking

Use external visual evidence to inform art direction without copying a single competitor.

## When to run
Run when materially useful, especially for:
- a new campaign,
- a new hero/KV,
- a new platform/surface,
- a deliberate visual upgrade,
- no mature campaign visual system,
- unclear or contested visual direction.

Usually reuse an existing recent benchmark for:
- copy/price edits,
- routine adaptations,
- local revisions,
- additional outputs inside a stable approved campaign system.

## Two-track benchmark

Keep the two tracks separate during research and in the deliverable. A reference may inform both tracks only when it independently satisfies both roles.

### A. Market / Platform Benchmark
Study what appears to work in the **target platform + target category** context.

#### Search order
Prefer evidence in this order:
1. target platform + same category,
2. target platform + adjacent category with a similar purchase/visual logic,
3. same category on closely comparable domestic platforms when target-platform evidence is insufficient,
4. broader cross-platform market references only as supplementary context.

Do not substitute overseas or generic e-commerce references for target-platform evidence when the task explicitly names a platform.

#### Sample qualification
A page is not a useful market benchmark merely because it is relevant to the product category.

For each candidate, record:
- platform / store / source,
- category fit,
- date checked,
- accessibility status,
- visible market-performance signal when available,
- why the sample is representative enough to include.

Accessibility status:
- **VERIFIED** — the page/content was actually opened and inspected,
- **PARTIAL** — only part of the page/content was accessible,
- **UNVERIFIED** — known only from search result/snippet/secondary mention,
- **INACCESSIBLE** — cannot be inspected; do not use as primary visual evidence.

Performance evidence may include:
- visible sales / monthly sales,
- review count,
- category or platform ranking,
- bestseller / leaderboard status,
- official flagship-store status,
- other platform-visible indicators.

Do not call a sample “head”, “top”, “best-selling”, “high-performing”, or equivalent without comparable evidence. When public performance data is unavailable, label the sample as a **platform/category reference**, not as performance proof.

#### Sample set
When live research is available:
- prefer **3–5 qualified target-platform/category samples** before broadening the search,
- aim for **5–10 total useful samples across both benchmark tracks** when efficient,
- do not satisfy the sample count with low-quality, inaccessible, weakly related, or redundant pages.

Extract:
- platform-effective patterns,
- category conventions,
- trust/proof patterns,
- repeated clichés,
- crowded/saturated approaches,
- differentiation opportunities.

### B. Visual Excellence Benchmark
Study high-quality commercial / brand visual references to understand what execution quality could achieve beyond category convention.

These references do **not** need to be the target platform, but they must be visually inspectable and intentionally selected for execution quality.

Prefer references that demonstrate:
- strong product presentation,
- disciplined composition/hierarchy,
- material/light treatment,
- typography/graphic discipline,
- memorable visual mechanisms,
- brand-specific rather than template-like execution.

Overseas references are often appropriate here, but they should not be presented as evidence of target-platform commercial effectiveness unless they independently qualify for Track A.

Extract reusable principles rather than copied layouts.

## Benchmark deliverable

Keep the evidence structure visible enough that the design recommendation can be audited.

### Market / Platform section
For each selected sample, retain:
- source,
- platform,
- category fit,
- date,
- accessibility status,
- visible market/performance signal or NONE OBSERVED,
- observed visual pattern,
- why it matters to the target platform/category.

### Visual Excellence section
For each selected reference, retain:
- source,
- date,
- accessibility status,
- quality dimension being studied,
- reusable visual principle,
- what must not be copied literally.

### Synthesis
Only after both tracks are sufficiently established, synthesize:
- effective platform/category patterns,
- visual-excellence patterns,
- saturated clichés,
- differentiation opportunities,
- anti-patterns,
- implications for the proposed visual direction.

If one track is weak or unavailable, say so explicitly instead of presenting a complete-sounding benchmark.

## Decision trace
For any benchmark-derived decision that materially affects the visual direction, retain a compact trace:

**Evidence → Observed Pattern → Visual Decision → Expected Effect → Risk / Trade-off**

This is an internal audit trail, not a requirement to expose hidden reasoning in CLIENT MODE.

## Anti-copy rule
Do not recreate one competitor as the design answer. Abstract principles across multiple references, then derive a product-specific direction.

If live research is unavailable, use supplied references and clearly mark the evidence gap.
